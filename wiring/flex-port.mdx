FLEX port wiring connects current transformer (CT) clamps to the FLEX CT Card module installed in the CSM device. CT clamps are the primary means of measuring current on the Cortex PQM — without correctly wired and configured CTs, the device cannot calculate power, energy, or power factor. Take care with polarity and with CT selection before you begin terminating wires.

## Supported CT Output Types

The FLEX CT Card module accepts two CT output signal types. Verify which output type your CT clamps use before connecting them.

| Output Type | Voltage Range | Common Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| 333mV output | 0–333mV AC | Most panel monitoring CTs; most common type |
| 0–5V output | 0–5V AC | Less common; typically higher-current industrial CTs |

<Note>
  Do not mix 333mV and 0–5V CT types on the same FLEX CT Card without confirming that the card supports per-channel output type selection. Refer to the CT Card datasheet for your specific module version.
</Note>

## CT Clamp Wiring to the CT Card Module

Each CT clamp produces a low-voltage signal that you wire directly to the CT Card module's terminal block. Most CT clamps ship with two wires: one labeled **K** (or **+**, or white) and one labeled **L** (or **−**, or black). Polarity determines the measured direction of current flow — getting it wrong does not damage the device, but it will cause the CSM to report negative power values.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the CT wire labels">
    Locate the **K** (positive) and **L** (negative) leads on your CT clamp. If your CT uses color coding instead of labels, white is typically K (+) and black is typically L (−). Check your CT clamp's datasheet to confirm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the CT clamp around the conductor">
    Clamp the CT around the conductor you want to measure. The arrow or label on the CT housing typically indicates the direction of positive current flow — orient the CT so that current flows in the direction the device expects (usually toward the load).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the K (+) wire">
    Insert the **K** (white/+) wire into the **K** terminal for the appropriate channel on the CT Card module terminal block. Tighten the captive screw until firm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the L (−) wire">
    Insert the **L** (black/−) wire into the **L** terminal for the same channel. Tighten the captive screw until firm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat for each channel">
    Repeat steps 1–4 for each additional CT clamp and channel. Label each wire pair before moving on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Label each CT wire pair with the circuit name or channel number before you finish the installation. Once the panel cover is replaced, it is extremely difficult to trace individual CT wires.
</Tip>

## Wire Routing Best Practices

Following these routing guidelines improves measurement accuracy and reduces noise pickup:

- **Route CT wires away from high-voltage conductors** where possible. Parallel runs next to AC line voltage wires can induce noise into the low-level CT signal.
- **Keep CT wire runs short.** For 333mV output CTs, keep runs under 3 meters. Longer runs increase the risk of voltage drop and noise pickup that degrades accuracy.
- **Use shielded cable for longer runs.** If your installation requires CT wire runs longer than 3 meters, use shielded twisted-pair cable.
- **Ground the shield at the CSM end only.** Connect the cable shield drain wire to ground at the CT Card module and leave it unconnected at the CT clamp. Grounding both ends creates a ground loop.

## CT Ratio Configuration

After completing physical wiring, you must configure the CT ratio for each channel in the CSM configuration file. The `ct_ratio` setting tells the device how to scale the measured voltage signal back to a current value in amperes.

```yaml
channels:
  - id: 1
    ct_ratio: 100        # Example: 100A CT with 333mV output
  - id: 2
    ct_ratio: 200        # Example: 200A CT with 333mV output
```

Set `ct_ratio` to the rated primary current of your CT clamp (in amperes). For example, a CT rated "100A / 333mV" uses a `ct_ratio` of `100`.

<Warning>
  Do not open-circuit a CT secondary while the primary conductor is energized. An open-circuited CT secondary can develop dangerously high voltages. Always ensure the CT wires are connected to the CT Card module terminal block — or the CT leads are shorted together — before energizing the primary conductor.
</Warning>
